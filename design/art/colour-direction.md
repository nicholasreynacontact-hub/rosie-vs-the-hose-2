# Colour: playful and psychedelic, and the Booga turn

The user, after the cartoon-to-3D rebuild landed:

> *"I need it to be more playful, almost psychedelic in how colorful the Rosie
> world is. And when it becomes Booga time, it becomes more of purple and reddish,
> kind of malicious vibe."*

## This supersedes the chroma rule, but not the ad-game standard

`not-an-ad-game.md` says *"cut chroma hard — one or two saturated accents only."*
That was written to fix a specific failure (magenta sky, coral deck and lime lawn
all shouting at once) and the user has now asked for the opposite direction. The
rule is replaced by the one below. **The ad-game test in that document still
stands** — the point was never that colour is bad, it was that *undifferentiated*
colour is bad.

The distinction, which is what this whole pass turns on:

| | Ad-game colour | Psychedelic colour |
|---|---|---|
| Saturation | Everything at maximum, flat | High, but *modulated* — things step forward and back |
| Hue | Every hue present, unrelated | Wide range, but deliberately related: analogous runs, planned complements |
| Value | Uniformly bright, no structure | Full range kept — a bright hue can still be a dark value |
| Shadows | Grey or black | Coloured — shadows take a hue of their own, usually the complement |
| Where it sits | Sprayed evenly over everything | Composed — the eye is led somewhere |

Max-saturating every object is the ad-game failure *again*, just louder. What
makes a frame read as psychedelic rather than cheap is **hue movement**: colour
shifting across a surface, shadows carrying their own hue, unexpected but
deliberate neighbours. Dreamy and saturated, with structure underneath.

## Daytime world

- **Keep the value structure that already works.** The garden reads well; this is
  a hue and saturation pass over it, not a relighting.
- **Push hue variety hard, saturation selectively.** Foliage moves through teal,
  jade, chartreuse and a little violet rather than sitting on one green. Flowers
  carry real saturation. Wood warms toward amber and rose.
- **Colour the shadows.** Shadow sides shift toward violet and deep teal instead
  of darkening in place. This single change does more for the psychedelic read
  than raising saturation anywhere.
- **Gradients across large surfaces.** The sky, the lawn and the mountains shift
  hue across their span rather than being one flat colour.
- **Rosie stays the lightest, least saturated thing in frame.** She is the read.
  The world goes colourful *around* her; she does not join in. The wardrobe's fur
  colours are the player's business, not this pass's.
- **The weak spot and the red hot band must still win.** As the rest of the frame
  gains saturation, those two have to be re-checked and pushed further, or the
  fight stops being readable. Non-negotiable.

## Booga time: purple and red, malicious

Booga currently goes purple and green and reads as a party. The user wants it to
turn **menacing**: purple and reddish.

- Sky to deep violet and plum, with red bleeding in at the horizon.
- Light turns hostile: a red-magenta key, violet shadow, and the lawn and deck
  picking up that cast instead of staying daytime green.
- Keep the chickens, fish and sharks — the absurdity is the joke — but light them
  in the same hostile palette so they read as ominous rather than cheerful.
- Rosie is the exception again: she stays warm and light against it, so a
  frightening sky makes her *more* visible, not less.
- The turn should be a visible event. The transition sells the mode.

## The test

Two frames, side by side: daytime and Booga. Daytime should read playful, dreamy
and colour-rich. Booga should read like something has gone wrong with the world.
If Booga just looks like daytime with the hue wheel spun, it has failed.
